## What separated them

**All three** produced a structurally sound file: 13/13 widgets, `/Opt`
exports byte-identical, identifiers kept, no invented glossary. The
mechanical pipeline is not what distinguishes a model.

**The two axes that split them are the two that need judgement.**

*Lookup.* Opus and Fable searched and named the real bilingual district
forms they took register from. Haiku wrote `none (not searched)` — honest,
and honesty is the difference between 0 and a lie, but it skipped the step
the skill says is mandatory when you can search.

*Visual pass.* Opus measured baselines and x-origins line by line and found
a 3.06 pt drift. Fable rendered at 220 dpi and reported the dropdown
caveat. Haiku rendered both pages, built the comparison HTML, then wrote
*"layout matches original pixel-faithfully"* over a page where the
signature caption hangs off the rule it labels. Rendering is not looking.

**Cost is not the story.** Haiku was 3× cheaper than Fable and 7× faster
than Opus, and its output would ship. The two points it lost are the two
that decide whether a translation is *right* rather than *placed*.

## Three defects, found by models rather than invented

1. **The placement gate could not pass a wrapped merge** (Opus). Any
   multi-line merge failed an otherwise-perfect build, which broke
   paragraph mode on the day it shipped. **Fixed**, with tests — a gate
   that fails correct output is the bug.
2. **The notice and the leak scan contradict each other** (Fable).
   `compliance.md` tells the author to name the source form title in the
   notice; the leak scan reads that English title as untranslated running
   text. Fable allowlisted and disclosed it. Needs its own sitting.
3. **List markers lose the source's gap** (Opus). `marker + ' '` where the
   source had two spaces shifts every list body 3.06 pt left. Needs its own
   sitting; the fix touches the segment schema.

And one the skill caused rather than suffered:

4. **`center` is documented for "signature captions"** — the one case where
   it is wrong, because those are left-anchored under a rule. Haiku followed
   the example and shipped a visible regression that every gate passed.
   The example is the defect. **Fixed** the same day as row 18: example
   corrected, `SKILL.md` step 5 says it, a constructed fixture locks the
   geometry.

## What this says about the skill

The structural gates work: three models, three different approaches, no
structural escapes. What they cannot do is notice that a caption is
anchored to something, that a notice's own title is not a leak, or that a
list body used to line up. Every defect above is a placement or policy
question that looks perfect to a gate reading strings.

That is the same conclusion the program already holds — a human looks at
the pages — but it is now evidence rather than assertion.
