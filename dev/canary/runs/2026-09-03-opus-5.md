## What it walked into

1. **Ligatures in a merged paragraph** (row 24). Its first build wrote
   `o\x7fcina` into the text layer: the Story engine applied the `fi`
   ligature and, with a subset font that had lost the ligature's code
   point, MuPDF reported U+007F. `canonicalize_text_layer` cannot reach a
   glyph no single code point maps to, and U+007F is not in the drift
   list, so only the verbatim `--translations` comparison caught it — and
   nothing would have without that flag. It stripped `liga`, `clig`,
   `dlig`, `rlig` and `hlig` from Noto Sans before subsetting. Sonnet hit
   the same defect the same evening and reworded around it. Reproduced on
   a constructed merge with the full font: five U+FB01 in the layer.
2. **The unreported shrink band** (row 25). Its delivery and checklist
   say "nothing shrank" and "allow_scale is EMPTY". Measured: the merged
   intro is **9.11 pt against 10 pt (0.91×)**. `retypeset` reports a
   scale only below 0.7×; between 0.7× and 1.0× it says nothing, so an
   author who trusts the tool's silence writes exactly this sentence.
   Sonnet's same merge shipped at 0.81× with the same silence.
3. **The notice channel** (P7). `place_notice.py`, its own script; the
   notice text was put into `translations.json` so the font subset and
   the gates cover it — the cleanest of the three workarounds seen tonight.
4. **A merge that needs one more line** (row 26). Two Spanish lines did
   not fit the two-line box at full size; the engine shrank them rather
   than take a third line into the empty space below.
