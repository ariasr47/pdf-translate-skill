# pdf-translate — deep review, 4 September 2026

Written against `metadata.version` 47 (commit `0a893c2`, 225 tests, CI green
on Linux and Windows under Python 3.10 and 3.13), after three canary runs and
with rows 01–31 closed. It reads the whole record — `HANDOVER.md`,
`PROGRAM.md`, the 2 September review, `SKILL.md` and its eight references,
the canary and its runs, the wild-corpus analysis, the eleven scripts — and
then does the one thing the record had never done: **it runs the whole
pipeline, retypeset and verify included, over real public documents.** No
model authored anything. Every core was pseudo-localised (diacritic-marked,
identifiers kept verbatim, padded to an expansion factor) so that every gate
runs exactly as it would on a real translation while the placement engine's
own error is measured on its own. Twenty-six such runs, plus fourteen
constructed fixtures driven through the shipped scripts by import, are the
evidence below. Nothing in the repository was edited; the instruments are in
the appendix so the numbers can be re-derived.

Interpreter for everything here: `pdf-translate/.venv/bin/python` (3.14.6,
PyMuPDF 1.28.2, pikepdf 10.12.0, fontTools 4.64.0).

## 1. Verdict

**The bet is right for the class it was made for, and the record has been
measuring the wrong thing.**

Right: on real fillable government forms, strip-and-retypeset holds end to
end. The USCIS N-400 — 14 pages, 440 widgets, 427 tooltips and dropdowns
translated through the widget channel — builds and passes all 26 verify
gates at zero expansion; the I-9 does the same and survives `field_fonts`.
Field parity, `/Opt` parity, the fill round-trip and the widget-text channel
all work at a scale the four-page fixtures never reached. The refusal
architecture, provider neutrality and the loop are sound; nobody else in the
market preserves forms (§4.3), and that is still the moat.

Wrong thing measured: three days of canaries measured **authors**, and the
wild corpus measured **strip and extract**. Nothing measured the placement
engine on a real document until today, and its error at identity is not
zero. With a metric-identical font, 27 of the W-9's 731 runs ship below
source size for no reason but a 1.5 pt pad (F3). The IRS booklet cannot
build at identity at all: 177 lines fail the 0.7× floor because the
extractor takes a line's size from its first span, and the first span is a
15 pt bullet (F2). A table cell's translation runs straight through the
vertical rule beside it, with every gate green, because retypeset's obstacle
scan never looks at drawings (F1). Every one of these is a defect a
translation inherits before the author has typed a word, and every one is
invisible to the canary because the canary's fixture is single-span,
rule-free and Helvetica.

The strongest argument against the project as it describes itself is not a
defect but a claim. `SKILL.md`'s description promises "any born-digital
PDF … pixel-faithful", and names manuals, brochures and contracts. The
end-to-end numbers say the architecture has **no vertical outlet for
expansion**: on the W-9 at +30 %, 463 of 731 runs ship shrunk and 201 of
them at 0.7–0.8×; on the arXiv paper at +30 %, 55 lines fail the build
outright; on the GDPR the merged paragraphs survive +30 % only by dropping to
0.70–0.80× type. For a form of short labels that is the right trade and the
skill says so. For a dense flow document into an expanding language, this
pipeline is a shrink-to-fit engine, and the delivery ought to say that in
the first sentence, not in a scale report.

So: keep the architecture, narrow the claim, and change what the program
measures. The next bar is not another gate a model walked into; it is
**"identity builds clean on all seventeen wild files"** — zero FAIL and
nothing below 0.9× with a metric-compatible face. That single dev-only
instrument (§5, row P8) would have found thirteen of the findings below
before any canary ran, and it turns the wild corpus from a strip/extract
probe into a measurement of the product.

## 2. Architecture — what the end-to-end runs measured, and the walls

### 2.1 The runs

Method (appendix A): strip with pseudo-localised captions and widget text,
extract, `from-cores`, fill every core with a diacritic-marked copy of
itself (`a→á, e→é, s→š …`; quoted spans, `Form …` names and URLs kept
verbatim, exactly as the skill requires), pad every word by the expansion
factor **E**, optionally `propose-merges --accept` with the paragraph text
pseudo-localised the same way, then `retypeset`, `verify --translations`,
and for two forms `field_fonts` and a second verify. `noto` is Noto Sans
Regular/Bold/Italic (the skill's recommended family); `arial` is Arial
Regular/Bold/Italic/Bold Italic (fsType 8, metric-compatible with the
Helvetica these forms are set in), used to separate font drift from
pipeline error. Times are wall-clock on the M1 Pro.

Two things the table settles that the record listed as open:

- **H-D, scale.** Strip 11 s, extract 25 s, retypeset 11 s per build, verify
  49 s on 126 pages with 374 Story-engine merges. Placement is not the
  bottleneck; verify's invisible-text oracle (a strip plus two renders per
  page) is, and it is fine.
- **The widget channel at scale.** 427 fields on the N-400 and 125 on the
  I-9 round-trip through `widget_text.json` and `--widget-text`, `/Opt`
  parity byte-identical, fill round-trip green. That was unmeasured; it
  works.

What Noto costs: Noto Sans is 6.2 % wider than Helvetica for the same
string, so every identity run with it reports a hundred or four thousand
runs in the 0.9–1.0 band that Arial does not. The scale report cannot tell
that drift from expansion (F21). With Arial the W-9's residue is 27 runs,
and those are pipeline error (F3).

### 2.2 The walls

These are limits of strip-and-retypeset itself — deleting text at the
content-stream level and re-inserting it at the same coordinates — not of
this implementation. No row removes them; the honest product names them.

**W1 — Expansion has no vertical outlet.** A run's budget is the room to
the next thing on its row; a paragraph's budget is its own box. Nothing
below ever moves. So a form absorbs growth by shrinking type (W-9 +30 %:
63 % of runs shrunk, 27 % of them to 0.7–0.8×) and a flow document absorbs
it only inside merged paragraphs (GDPR +30 %: fifteen paragraphs at
0.70–0.80×) while every unmerged body line fails (arXiv +30 %: 55). A
paragraph that continues on the next page cannot be reflowed at all —
merges are per page. **Class that hits the wall:** dense running text into
a language that expands (EN→ES/FR/DE/RU manuals, contracts, regulations,
papers) unless the requester accepts smaller type. The skill's "reword
first" is the right answer for forty labels and no answer for four hundred
lines.

**W2 — Tagged and accessible output is impossible.** The structure tree is
deleted because nothing can rebuild it (pikepdf still has no structure-tree
API, PyMuPDF none; §4.2). Thirteen of the seventeen wild files are tagged
and ten carry `/Lang`. Under the DOJ's Title II rule (WCAG 2.1 AA, compliance
by 26 April 2027 for entities over 50,000 people, 2028 below; courts are
covered entities) and the European Accessibility Act (in force 28 June
2025), a translated form a public body **publishes** must be tagged. **Class
that hits the wall:** anything a government or court will post. The output
is a reading copy for one person, and the delivery text should say that
rather than "no longer tagged".

**W3 — Text that is not ink, or ink that is not text.** A headline used as
a clipping path floods its fill across the page once the clip is deleted
(F16, 2,902 → 80,000 dark pixels: the whole page). Text drawn in render
mode 3 is invisible in the source and visible in the output. A 25 % opacity
watermark comes back opaque. Stroked text comes back filled. The strip
deletes semantics it cannot reproduce because it only knows `BT…ET`.
Detection is cheap (F16); reproduction is not possible.

**W4 — Hyperlink hotspots do not follow reflowed text** (F7). A `/Link`'s
rect is fixed; a merged paragraph moves its words. Medicare & You has 1,607
links, the IRS booklet 498, the paper 119. Nothing in the pipeline reads a
link rect except the RTL mirror.

**W5 — Dynamic XFA has nothing to translate** (F14). The page tree is a
"Please wait…" placeholder and the form lives in XML the pipeline deletes.
IRCC's IMM forms are still this class in 2026 (§4.4).

**W6 — A signed document cannot stay signed** (F15). Any byte the pipeline
writes invalidates an approval signature the same way it invalidates
`/DocMDP`; the output is an unsigned copy, and today nothing says so.

**W7 — One family per role.** `fonts` carries weight and slant, not family.
A document that mixes a serif body, a sans heading, a condensed callout and
a monospace register name (the datasheet; the IRS booklet with its
Helvetica Neue Condensed callouts, Times body and Helvetica World labels)
comes back in one family, and every pass-through numeral, date and amount
comes back in Helvetica whatever the source (67 % of the booklet's segments,
75 % of the W-4's). "Visually indistinguishable" is bounded by this before
any translation happens.

**W8 — Symbol glyphs.** Every real file I pushed end to end was stopped at
the glyph gate by a character Noto Sans does not carry — `●` on the I-9,
`►` on the N-400, `✓ ✗ 📄 ✨` on the paper, one on the datasheet — and the
only exits the format offers are `skip` (the glyph vanishes) or a font the
author merges by hand. Worse, a ZapfDingbats check mark is not stopped at
all: it is the character "4" in that font's encoding, and it ships as a
Helvetica digit 4 with every gate green (F5).

**W9 — Layers.** Text inside an optional-content group that is off by
default is deleted by strip and never seen by extract (constructed; not yet
seen in the wild, §5 hypotheses). Bilingual layered PDFs and print-only
instructions are the class.

## 3. Findings

Severity: **high** = fails a build at identity, or ships a wrong page with
every gate green, on a real document class; **medium** = a false FAIL or a
silent gap an author will hit on real forms; **low** = a claim the scripts
do not honour, or cosmetic. "Proven" means a fixture driven through the
shipped scripts by import; the reproduction commands are in appendix B.

### F1 — A vertical rule is not an obstacle; a table cell's translation crosses it — high, proven

`retypeset.right_limit` (and `left_limit`) bound a run by the next segment
and the next widget on its row and by the page margin. They never read the
page's drawings, although `extract_segments.page_obstacles` already
collects every vertical rule and box edge for the `right-aligned` warning.

Fixture: a two-column table with a vertical rule at x = 200, "Description"
and "Fee" in the left cells, "Amount" in the right header and the right body
cell empty (`I_rule.pdf`). Translation of "Fee" → *Tasa administrativa anual
del expediente*.

```
[I] retypeset exit 0
[I] "Tasa" bbox x1 = 257.7 (rule at x=200)
[I] "Descripción" bbox x1 = 248.1 (rule at x=200)
[I] scale_report: []
[I] verify exit 0
```

Both cells run 48–58 pt through the rule; nothing shrank, nothing failed;
the render shows the text crossing the line. On real forms, counting from
each file's own `segments.json` and `page_obstacles`: FL-100 **46 of 188**
translatable segments (24 %), W-9 **107 of 712** (15 %), N-400 **70 of 845**
(8 %), I-9 **252 of 349** (72 %) have a vertical rule closer on their right
than anything retypeset would stop at, with a median 4–6 pt of phantom room
and, wherever the neighbouring cell is empty, the whole cell.

### F2 — A line's size is its first span's size; a 15 pt bullet makes the whole line 15 pt — high, proven on the IRS booklet

`extract_segments` merges a line's spans into one group and keeps the first
span's `size`. In the IRS 1040 instructions the bullet `•` is drawn in a
15 pt Times span and the sentence after it in 9–10 pt. Retypeset then draws
the whole line at 15 pt: Noto width 771 pt in a 520 pt budget, so 0.67×, and
177 such lines fail the 0.7× floor **at identity**. The build cannot succeed
on that document without `allow_scale`ing 164 keys.

```
p3 0.67x '• You’ve tried to contact the IRS but no one '
    seg bbox w=443.5 size=15.0 noto w=771.3 maxw=519.9 limit from page margin | source fonts {'TimesNewRomanPSMT'}
p5 0.64x '• No tax on tips. You may be eligi-'
    seg bbox w=151.6 size=15.0 noto w=235.9 maxw=160.1
```

### F3 — A run shrinks at identity because of its own pad — low, proven on the W-9

With Arial (metric-identical to the W-9's Helvetica), 27 of 731 runs still
ship at 0.92–0.98×. "The actual owner" (p5, 53.8 pt wide in the source) has
a superscript "1" starting at x = 232.59 where the run ends at 232.6;
`right_limit` subtracts 1.5 pt from that neighbour and the run must shrink to
0.953× to fit a budget narrower than the width it already had. The budget
of a run should never be less than the width the source drew it at.

### F4 — The canonical text-layer rewrite covers only the regular and bold faces — high, proven on FL-100 and N-400

`retypeset.py` line 643: `fontfiles = [fonts['regular'], fonts.get('bold', …)]`.
A run drawn with the italic or bold-italic face keeps MuPDF's reverse-mapped
`/ToUnicode`. Any font whose cmap maps NBSP and space (or soft hyphen and
hyphen) to one glyph — Arial does, Noto does not — then reports drift, and
the always-on canonical gate FAILs a correct build, and the placement gate
cannot find the verbatim target.

Fixture `F_italic.pdf` (a Helvetica-Oblique span; fonts regular = Arial,
italic = Arial Italic):

```
[F] verify exit 1: FAIL text layer is not canonical (2 character(s) nobody authored …)
   U+00A0 NO-BREAK SPACE x4 | U+00AD SOFT HYPHEN x1 | FAIL missing translation targets (1)
   span 'Sub\xadtotal\xa0de\xa0todos\xa0los\xa0artículos' font Arial-ItalicMT
```

With the italic aliased to the regular face (`F2`, what `from-cores`
scaffolds) it passes, which is why 225 tests and three canaries never saw
it. On the real FL-100 with Arial + Arial Italic at identity: `U+00A0 ×279`
and `missing translation targets (26)`; on the N-400: 2 characters, 22
targets. `fonts.md` tells Windows users Arial "has both scripts for tests".

### F5 — Symbol glyphs: a check mark ships as "4", and bullets stop every real build — high, proven

(a) A ZapfDingbats check mark is the byte "4" in that font. MuPDF extracts
"4"; the extractor's `PASS` regex makes it pass-through; retypeset draws it
in Helvetica. Output text layer `'4 | Sí, acepto'`, span font
`NimbusSans-Regular`, every gate green, and the page shows the digit 4 where
the tick was (render `E3_after.png`). ZapfDingbats/Dingbats are in three of
the seventeen wild files (arXiv, Medicare, DS-11 with Wingdings and Symbol).

(b) A `☐`, `●`, `►`, `✓`, `✗` or emoji in a core fails the glyph check
against Noto Sans (correctly), but the format has no fallback face: the
author either `skip`s the segment (the glyph and its words vanish) or builds
a merged font. In practice four of the six real documents pushed end to end
could not build until such segments were skipped — 3 on the I-9, 2 on the
N-400, 4 on the paper, 1 on the datasheet. The glyph digest also names only
the **first** run per (page, role, character), so the author learns the
rest one build at a time.

### F6 — Merges: three defects on real flow documents — high, proven

(a) **Proposals overlap.** `propose_merges` folds in both `merge-candidate`
and `narrow-column` groups, which share segments (a table column is both).
On the arXiv paper, `propose-merges --accept` as shipped yields one overlap
and retypeset stops with `!! merge not matched p0: Feature (1/7)` — a line
that is not even prefixed `FAIL`. On the booklet: 10 overlapping proposals.

(b) **Merges are matched by line text, first hit wins, in extraction
order.** A page with the same line twice (tables, "Yes/No" columns) or with
blocks extracted out of reading order cannot build its merge:
`!! merge not matched p28: Enter the total pension … (3/6)`. On the
booklet's 446 proposals, 60 have a line whose text is duplicated on the page
and 2 have lines out of extraction order; all 62 are unbuildable as
proposed, although `segments.json` and the proposals both know the segment
ids.

(c) **A heading is swallowed by the paragraph under it.** `merge_candidate_warnings`
checks column, vertical spacing and an open sentence end, never size. On
Medicare & You page 3 the 31 pt "Contents" and the sixteen 11 pt table-of-
contents lines beneath it (dot leaders included) are one candidate; accepted,
the Story engine fits them at 0.42× and the build fails at identity.

### F7 — A link's hotspot stays where the source word was — medium, proven

Fixture `C_link.pdf`: a two-line paragraph with a `/Link` on "website",
merged into a three-line Spanish paragraph with an authored `box`.

```
[C] link rect [40.0, 62.2, 77.3, 77.3] uri kept
[C] text under the link rect BEFORE: 'Read th\nwebsite' | AFTER: 'publicad\nfirmar e'
[C] where "sitio web" landed: [[119.5, 60.3, 164.8, 75.0]]
[C] verify exit 0
```

Nothing reads links except the mirror; verify has no link gate at all, not
even a count. Wild corpus: 1,607 links on Medicare & You, 498 on the
booklet, 119 on the paper, 35 on the datasheet, 28 on the 1040-ES, 22 on the
G-28.

### F8 — Field rects are not gated, although the skill says they are — medium, proven

`SKILL.md` ("field names/types/rects stay identical") and `HANDOVER.md` §3
("names, types, rects") against `verify.py`: the parity set is (fully
qualified name, type). A copy of the canary fixture with one text widget
moved by (+80, −40) pt: `PASS field parity`. `gates.md` says name and type
only, so the three documents disagree with each other and one of them is
wrong about what is locked.

### F9 — Captions are keyed by the annotation's partial `/T`, so LiveCycle forms collide — high, proven on FL-100

`strip_text` matches `--captions` keys against `a.get('/T')` and its
`[0]`-less base. Every Designer form is hierarchical: FL-100's five
form-number link buttons are all `Button1[0]` or `Button1[1]` with different
parents and different captions (`FL-105`, `Attachment 9b.`, `FL-160`,
`Attachment 10b.`, `form FL-107-INFO`). `--captions {"Button1": "XX"}`
rewrites **six** buttons on three pages to `XX`, and there is no key that
addresses one of them. `verify` prints the fully qualified name
(`FL-100[0].Page1[0].Button1[0]`), which strip does not accept. The chrome
gate is then satisfied by the collision.

### F10 — The caption-width gate fails the issuer's own captions — medium, proven on FL-100

At identity, six of FL-100's ten pushbuttons FAIL gate 10 untouched:
`FL-105` at 9 pt in a 28.5 pt-wide button (Helvetica width 27.5 + 4 pt pad),
and `Warning[0]`, a 211 × 23 pt button whose caption is a two-line sentence
at 8 pt — Acrobat wraps it; the gate measures one line. The gate compares
the output caption against the rect only, never against what the source
caption already was.

### F11 — The identifier gate fails every quoted or `Form` span that wraps a line — medium, proven on the W-9 and the booklet

`collect_identifier_spans` runs the write/find/say regexes over
`page.get_text()`, where a span can contain a newline; the comparison is
`normalize_ws_nbsp`, which folds NBSP and hyphens but not newlines. At
identity: W-9 four misses (`'Form \n1040'`, `'By signing the filled-\nout
form'` …), booklet **93**, all 93 containing a line break. The output has
`Form 1040` with one space.

### F12 — The identifier gate demands what the leak scan then fails — medium, proven on the W-9

Item 3b quotes “Partnership” and “Trust/estate,”. The identifier gate
requires both verbatim; the leak scan tokenises them into three consecutive
source words and FAILs `p1: Partnership Trust estate`. The only escape is
`--allow` with a phrase per instance, which the canary fixture never needed
because its quoted spans are two words long.

### F13 — Widget-text scaffold: empty options cannot be authored, parent tooltips are invisible — medium, proven

An `/Opt` entry whose display string is empty (the blank first row of a
State dropdown) is scaffolded with `target: null`; `null` refuses the build
and an empty target is refused too, so the author's only move is to delete
the entry, which nothing says. I-9: 5 such options; N-400: 4. Separately,
`widget_text_scaffold` reads `/FT` and `/TU` from the annotation only: a
tooltip held on the parent field is never offered. SF-15: 8 fields, I-9: 4,
DS-11: 2.

### F14 — A dynamic XFA form is translated, not refused — high, proven by construction

`/AcroForm /NeedsRendering true`, an XFA packet, and a page whose only text
is "Please wait… If this message is not eventually replaced…". Strip:
`xfa_removed True`. Extract: 3 cores, no refusal. The pipeline would
translate the placeholder. Nothing reads `/NeedsRendering`.

### F15 — An applied signature is invalidated and nothing says so — medium, proven by construction

A `/Sig` field with a `/V` signature dictionary: strip's report has no key
for it; the output carries the `/V` with a `ByteRange` over bytes that no
longer exist; `PASS field parity`. The corpus has no signed file, but
"translate this signed contract" is an ordinary request.

### F16 — Render mode and opacity are not read, although MuPDF now reports them — medium, proven by construction

PyMuPDF 1.28's span dict carries `char_flags` (filled 16, stroked 32,
clipped 64) and `alpha`. A constructed page with the four modes:

```
'visible fill'         char_flags=16 alpha=255   source 502 dark px → output 7,208
'invisible mode three' char_flags=0  alpha=0     source   0         → output 15,980
'stroked only'         char_flags=32 alpha=255   source 1,387       → output 9,690
'clip only'            char_flags=80 alpha=0     source 427         → output 6,494
```

All four become cores and are drawn filled. The clip case also floods its
fill (`A_textclip.pdf`, whole page blue). A 25 % opacity "DRAFT COPY"
(`alpha=77`) comes back at 100 % (darkest pixel 192 → 0). The I-9 carries
nine render-mode-3 blocks in its page content (four with words such as
"First Name (Given Name) from Section 1."); the annotation-free display list
did not surface them, so on that file the defect did not fire — the reason
is not established (§6).

### F17 — XMP `dc:title` and `dc:description` keep the source — low, proven

`apply_document_metadata` writes the Info `/Title` through
`Document.set_metadata`; XMP is touched only for `dc:language`. After
retypeset: Info `Formulario de solicitud de permiso`, XMP `dc:title` `Permit
Application Form`, `PASS document metadata`. Fourteen of the seventeen wild
files carry an XMP title equal to their Info title; PDF 2.0 deprecates the
Info dictionary in favour of XMP and preflight tools flag the mismatch
(§4.5).

### F18 — `/Lang` loses the region subtag — low, proven

`Document.set_language('es-MX')` → `/Lang es`; likewise `zh-Hant-TW → zh`,
`pt-BR → pt`, `sr-Latn → sr`. `retarget_xmp` writes the full tag into
`dc:language`, so the file disagrees with itself. verify tolerates it by
prefix. `SKILL.md` step 5 and `translations-format.md` both promise the
BCP-47 tag lands in `/Lang`.

### F19 — The skill text, where it is wrong or contradicts the scripts — low to medium

- **The hot-loop block omits `--translations`.** `SKILL.md` lines 100–103
  (`pipeline.py rebuild … --fill-text … --source-words-from segments.json`)
  is the command an agent copies; without `--translations` verify prints 7
  gate lines instead of 14 — no placement, no chrome, no caption width, no

## 4. Research — what the outside world knows

### 4.1 Libraries and the pins

- **PyMuPDF 1.28.2** (6 August 2026) is the newest release; there is no
  1.29. Since 1.26: linearization removed (1.26.0, `linear=True` raises),
  `Document.apply_css()` (1.28.0), `TEXT_CLIP` and other extraction flags
  (1.27.1), `find_tables` refinements (1.28.2), MuPDF < 1.26 dropped
  (1.27.1). **`pymupdf>=1.24,<1.30` holds.** What the repo does not use and
  should: the span dict's `char_flags` and `alpha` (F16), `TEXT_ALIGN_JUSTIFY`
  is a constant the Story engine honours (F19) [1].
- **MuPDF 1.28.3** (22 August 2026) is the newest engine release [2].
- **pikepdf 10.13.0** is the newest; the local venv has 10.12.0. Since
  10.9: `StreamParser` public (10.12.1), typed XMP property handling
  (10.13.0), JobBuilder, annotation transforms; minimum qpdf 12.3.2; Python
  3.10–3.14. Version 11 is announced only as the release that removes
  positional arguments to `Page.rotate()`. **`pikepdf>=8,<11` holds**, and
  nothing in the 10.x notes touches structure trees, so the tag-rebuild
  parking stands [3].

### 4.2 Accessibility: the tag deletion has a deadline attached

- ISO 14289-2:2024 (**PDF/UA-2**) was published March 2024, built on
  PDF 2.0, requiring tagged structure, reading order, alternate text and
  form labels [4][5].
- The **European Accessibility Act** applies from 28 June 2025 to
  customer-facing documents of covered services, with a transition to 2030
  for existing ones [6].
- The **DOJ's ADA Title II rule** adopts WCAG 2.1 AA for state and local
  government web content and documents, courts included; an Interim Final
  Rule of 20 April 2026 moved the deadlines to **26 April 2027** (population
  ≥ 50,000) and **26 April 2028** (smaller) [7][8][9].

Implication: the honest sentence is not "the file is no longer tagged" but
"this file cannot be published by a public body"; W2 is a stated dead end
for that class until a library can rebuild tags, and none can.

### 4.3 The competition, September 2026

BabelDOC (the paper in the corpus is its own) preserves formulas, charts
and bookmarks, offers mono and dual output, a glossary CSV, an OCR
workaround, talks only to OpenAI-compatible endpoints, and lists `--skip-
form-render` among its flags; its own known-issues list says "Lines are not
supported" and "Does not support drop caps" [10]. pdf2zh 2.0 is its front
end [11]. Nobody preserves AcroForms; the niche is still empty. What they
have that this repo does not: a layout model that decides paragraph
membership (this repo's candidate heuristic is F6c), and an explicit dual
output that side-steps W1 by not promising the same page.

### 4.4 XFA, still

XFA is not part of PDF 2.0 (ISO 32000-2:2017) and is excluded from PDF/A
[12][13]. IRCC's IMM 5257 is still a dynamic XFA form in 2026 — pdf.js's
tracking issue is open ("XFA-editing is not implemented") and Adobe's
community threads are the "Please wait…" class [14][15]. F14 is a real
document class with a public example that the corpus could not fetch.

### 4.5 Metadata

The document information dictionary is deprecated and removed in PDF 2.0;
pikepdf's guidance is that both must carry the same content and its
`open_metadata()` keeps them in sync [16]. Preflight for PDF/X flags a
title mismatch between the two as an error [17]. F17 is one
`open_metadata()` call.

### 4.6 Translation standards

- **ISO 17100:2015** defines translation as translation plus revision by a
  second person; the skill cites it. A revision is under way (ISO/AWI
  17100, presented April 2025) to address technology [18][19].
- **ISO 18587:2017** is the standard for *full human post-editing of
  machine-translation output* and post-editor competences [20]. A mapping
  a model authors **is** MT output; the reviser the skill names is a
  post-editor under 18587, and that is the standard the delivery should
  cite beside 17100.
- Multi-line link hotspots have had `QuadPoints` since PDF 1.6, and
  Acrobat's own reflow cannot move annotations [21][22] — W4 is the
  industry's problem too, and the answer is quadpoints, not a rect.

### 4.7 Fonts

The OFL FAQ: embedding a font in a document, full or subset, is permitted
and does not trigger the Reserved Font Name clause, which binds
redistributed modified fonts, not documents [23]. `embedding_permission`'s
reading of `fsType` (bit 1 restricted, bits 2–3 preview/print and editable,
bit 8 no subsetting, bit 9 bitmap only) matches the OpenType specification.
What the skill lacks is F21: metric-compatible OFL faces so the report is
about the translation, not the font.

### 4.8 Eval automation

`claude plugin eval` is still early access; nothing public announces
general availability. P4 stays blocked on the grant, not on work.

    for kind, snippet in write_find_say_hits(text):
        for m in re.finditer(re.escape(snippet), text): keep.append((m.start(), m.end()))

        out.append(w + 'ø' * k if k > 0 else w)

        counts['empty_display_options_omitted'] += len(entry['options']) - len(opts)
        if opts: e['options'] = opts
    for k in ('value', 'default'):
        if k in entry: e[k] = entry[k]['source']         # data: identity, as widget-text.md says
    if e: wt[name] = e

for p in d:
    for w in p.widgets():
        if w.field_type_string == 'Button' and (w.field_flags & (1 << 16)) and w.button_caption:
            key = w.field_name if a.caption_key == 'full' else w.field_name.split('.')[-1]
            caps[key] = pseudo(w.button_caption, 1.0)
d.close()
stripped = os.path.join(work, 'stripped.pdf')
with contextlib.redirect_stdout(io.StringIO()): rep = strip_text(src, stripped, captions=caps, widget_text=wt)
t['strip'] = time.perf_counter() - t0; t0 = time.perf_counter()
assert not rep['leftover_text'], rep['leftover_text']
counts['captions_rewritten'] = len(rep['rewritten_captions'])
with contextlib.redirect_stdout(io.StringIO()): res = extract_segments(src, outdir=work)

    json.dump(conf, open(tr, 'w', encoding='utf-8'), ensure_ascii=False)
    with contextlib.redirect_stdout(io.StringIO()): propose_merges(work, accept=True)
    conf = json.load(open(tr, encoding='utf-8')); counts['merges_proposed'] = len(conf['merges'])
    seg_index = {}; cnt = Counter()
    for i, sg in enumerate(res['segments']):
        cnt[(sg['page'], sg['text'].strip())] += 1; seg_index.setdefault((sg['page'], sg['text'].strip()), i)
    used = set(); keep = []
    for m in conf['merges']:
        keys = [(m['page'], l.strip()) for l in m['lines']]
        if any(cnt[k] > 1 for k in keys): counts['merges_dropped_duplicate_line_text'] += 1; continue
        if set(keys) & used: counts['merges_dropped_overlapping_proposal'] += 1; continue

    for m in conf['merges']:
